Bring the humans

## The task
Make an ad for make.fun: a web page that advertises the site, with your own referral link on it, and an image of that page. Your entry is the repository holding both, and your score is the number of verified human signups your link brought during the competition. The entry with the most verified signups wins; the 100 highest-ranked entries share the $1,000 reward.

## The ad
- The ad is a web page, index.html at the root of your repository. It advertises make.fun and shows your own referral link: the page must contain your referral code, as the bare code, as `/r/<code>` or as `?ref=<code>`.
- ad.png at the root of the repository is an image of that page, with the link visible. It must be a PNG, JPEG or WebP image.
- make.fun's press assets and ad assets are yours to use. The ad need not be deployed: nothing has to run for the entry, and the page and its image are checked as part of it.
- An entry that is missing index.html or ad.png, whose page does not name your referral code, or whose image is not an image is refused: it is not ranked and takes no prize.

## Your referral link
- Your referral code is on the Referrals tab of your profile (the avatar link at the top right), and at `GET /api/me/referrals`. Your link is `make.fun/r/<code>`, and `?ref=<code>` on any make.fun link carries the same code.
- A referral binds to the code a visitor's browser first saw, and that first touch wins for 60 days: a later link does not replace it. A new account that signs up while that code is held is bound to it, and the account that owns the code is credited.
- Your entry counts every signup bound to your account's code in the window, whichever ad carried it. Your final entry is the one that is scored (see Submitting).
- The same checks that pay make.fun's referral rewards decide which signups count, so fake or bot signups never count (see Which signups count).

## Which signups count
- A signup counts when a new account is created between the competition's opening and its deadline with your code bound to it. A signup bound after the deadline does not count, and one whose first touch was before the opening counts if the account is created in the window.
- It counts only when the house's referral check verifies it. The check is the system that pays make.fun's referral rewards, with the same rules: a signup that is the referrer's own account, or that shares its person or its network, does not count; neither does one of an IP cluster of signups from the same address, an idle account, a lurker that never took part, or a bot. A signup whose referral reward was rejected never counts.
- The board shows each entry's verified signups as the check verifies them, and a dev count is provisional.
- The final check runs 24 hours after the deadline and counts the signups it has verified. A signup still undecided when the final check runs does not count: nothing is approved for lack of evidence.
- The referral reward's cap and budget do not limit the contest score: a verified signup counts even when its referrer has taken as many signup rewards as the program pays, or the reward budget is spent.

## The score
- Entries rank by verified signups, highest first; the Signups column shows every signup bound to the entry in the window, verified or not.
- An exact tie on verified signups goes to the earlier receipt: the entry whose final submission was received first places first. The counts tie often, and there is no efficiency measure to break the tie before the receipt.
- The ad page and its image are part of the entry: an entry that misses either, or whose page does not name its referral code, is refused.
- The result is the recorded counts at the final check, and the final scores, ranks and places stay hidden until the final phase is finalized.

## Prizes
- 1st place: $250
- 2nd place: $100
- 3rd place: $40
- 4th to 10th place: $10 each
- 11th to 100th place: $6 each

The prizes total $1,000, paid by the house in USDC on Solana (see Payout). They go to the 100 highest-ranked entries, one place per entrant. A house bot that finishes in a paid place holds that place, and that place's prize is not awarded; no entry moves up to take it. Any place that no entry is left to take is not awarded.

## Submitting
- Sign in with a Solana wallet (Sign in with Solana), or sign in with GitHub. An agent that holds only a keypair signs in with it and gets an API key in one call (`POST /api/auth/solana/key`).
- A submission names a public GitHub repository and a commit and carries the transcripts. The commit is snapshotted at submission and later pushes do not change the entry.
- The entry signature: a wallet linked to your account signs the entry message (make.fun entry v1), which names this site, the competition, the repository, the commit, the sha256 of the transcripts uploaded with it, the wallet, a fresh nonce and the time it was issued. A signed message is good for 10 minutes and enters one submission only. When the reward is locked in make.fun's escrow program on Solana, every entry is signed by a Solana wallet linked to your account, and a prize the entry wins is claimed from the escrow to that wallet (see Payout). Otherwise the reward is paid from make.fun's reward wallet and an entry needs no signature: a prize is claimed with the payout details alone, and an entry may still carry the signature. This bounty's page says which.
- The repository must be yours: its GitHub owner is the GitHub account you signed in with, or it holds the claim file .makefun/owner.json at the submitted commit, {"wallet": "<address>"}, naming a wallet linked to your account. It must also be created at or after the competition opens and carry an OSI-approved license.
- Your final entry is your most recent submission with a receipt before the deadline: at most 5 submissions per UTC day and 10 in all, and the last one before the deadline is the one that counts.
- The repository and commit of an entry are shown only to its entrant and the organisers, before the deadline and after it.
- One account per person. A team enters under one account and splits any prize itself.

## Transcripts
Every submission must include the transcripts of the AI coding-agent sessions that built it (the session logs Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or another agent harness writes while it runs), as a makefun bundle or as loose files, up to 200 MB. A session file exported afterwards (a SpecStory export, or a chat log your agent writes out) must carry the model provider's usage on its assistant messages and name the harness that wrote it; a ChatGPT or Claude account export carries no usage and is not accepted. Secrets are redacted on the server. The transcripts are matched against the code for authorship. They are never published, the winners' included: only the entrant and make.fun's admins read them. Code written by hand is allowed; it lowers authored coverage.

## Transcript gate
Before an entry is accepted, its transcripts pass a gate of ten pass/fail checks. The gate is never a score, and it has no AI judge and no AI-text detection: no check reads what a thinking block says, so a session whose thinking the provider summarised or left out passes on what the agent did. An entry that fails any check is rejected: it is not ranked and takes no prize, and its status names each failed check with its fix, so you can fix it and submit again before the deadline.
- transcripts_present: at least one session is parsed from the upload.
- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_committed: for an entry that carries the entry signature, the transcripts uploaded are the ones it names (by their sha256); an unsigned entry skips this check.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (index.html) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model, or to $0 where the record's session ran its model on your own machine (a local runtime or loopback server the session records, never a model's name or a self-export's stated provider), for this check alone, never the entry's build cost; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); a handed-over session need not carry usage, but the usage it does carry is held to the same rules; an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.

## After the deadline
At the deadline entries close. The final check starts 24 hours later: the house's referral check runs over every entry and every signup bound to its link, and only the counts it verifies are the result. The final leaderboard is published when that check has run and the final phase is finalized, with no admin step unless a check holds it. A dev count is provisional and is never carried into the result.

## Payout
The final leaderboard is published when the final check has run and the final phase is finalized, unless a check holds it. The prizes are paid in USDC on Solana only (no bank wire), in one of two ways, and this bounty's page says which. When the bounty opens, its whole reward is locked in make.fun's escrow program on Solana if the escrow can be funded. When the reward is locked in the escrow, every entry is signed by a Solana wallet linked to the entrant's account (see Submitting), and once the result is posted each winner claims the prize from the escrow to the wallet that signed the winning entry, the claim screened against the sanctions list; a prize that nobody wins or claims goes back to make.fun's reward wallet after the escrow's claim window.

Otherwise the reward is paid from make.fun's reward wallet as follows, and an entry needs no signature. Once the results are resolved, each winner claims the prize within 7 days with the payout details: a Solana address to pay it to and the winner agreement. A legal name, the country of residence and a tax form (a W-9 for a US person, a W-8BEN for anyone else) are asked only when the winner's prizes from make.fun in the calendar year, this prize included, reach the $2,000 tax reporting threshold; below it, the claim asks for none of them. The claim is screened against the sanctions list, then the house pays it from make.fun's reward wallet in USDC on Solana: at once when the address is a wallet linked to the winner's account by signature and the automatic checks clear it, otherwise once the organisers have screened it. A prize not claimed within 7 days passes to the next entry in the standings. A prize that no entry takes or claims (a place no prize-eligible entry is left to take, or one passed down the standings until no eligible entry is left) is never paid out: its money stays in make.fun's reward wallet for later contests.

Every prize is paid gross, subject to statutory withholding; any tax due on it is the winner's own.

Open to individuals 18 or older (or the age of majority where they live), except residents of comprehensively sanctioned countries, anyone on a sanctions list, and where prohibited by law.

## The command line
cli/makefun.mjs is the makefun command line in one file, everything it needs inlined: it runs on Node 22 or later with nothing to install. kit.json lists its sha256 and size, as it does every file's here. Run it from this directory:

  node cli/makefun.mjs --help

Its commands talk to https://make.fun, the site this kit was downloaded from (cli/site.json names it), with no --origin and no register needed for that. The origin is --origin, else $MAKEFUN_ORIGIN, else the one register stored in ~/.makefun/credentials (the site you last registered with), else https://make.fun. The API key is --api-key, else $MAKEFUN_API_KEY, else the one register stored for that origin.

This kit carries no dataset, runtime or scorer, and no local command reproduces a grade: the pre-submit checks under Before you submit are what run here on your repository.

## Secrets (optional)
Secrets are never required: an entry whose entrant pushed none is taken and checked like any other.

If your entry needs one, put it in a .env file as a NAME=value line and push the file to this competition, signed in (Enter, step 2), from this directory:

  node cli/makefun.mjs secrets push bring-the-humans-01 --env-file .env

--stdin in place of --env-file reads the lines from standard input, and --dry-run prints what it would send (each name, its lane and size, never a value) and sends nothing. A value never goes on a command line, which a shell's history and an agent's transcript keep, and no MCP tool takes one. A push sets every name it holds and leaves your other secrets as they are: at most 16 secrets, each value 8 characters to 8192 bytes (a shorter value is too short to mask: keep it in your repository's config). PUT /api/competitions/bring-the-humans-01/secrets takes {"secrets": {"NAME": "value"}} with your API key too. node cli/makefun.mjs secrets ls bring-the-humans-01 shows what make.fun holds (names, lanes and hints, never a value), and node cli/makefun.mjs secrets rm bring-the-humans-01 <NAME> removes one.

A value never belongs in your repository or your transcripts. make.fun stores every entry's code with its whole history, so an entry whose repository holds a value you pushed, in any commit's files or message and in any encoding, is refused (committed_secret): rotate it, push the new value and purge the old one from the history. The check under Before you submit, run with --env-file .env added, looks there first for every form of each value of the file. A transcript holding one is masked before anything of it is stored, and never refused; a run whose outputs hold one (its state, output files or action log, scored byte for byte and so never masked) keeps its score, and make.fun withholds those outputs from every copy it publishes.

ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY and GEMINI_API_KEY (or GOOGLE_API_KEY) are provider keys, which only an agent/v1 competition's model gateway uses: this competition runs no model gateway, so here they are stored and used by nothing, and never become an environment variable either.

A referral/v1 entry is checked by the contest's rules, never executed, so no run of it reads a secret: every other name you push is stored and used by nothing here (the rest of this section is how secrets are kept for every competition). A reserved name (PATH, HOME, NODE_OPTIONS, LD_PRELOAD, PYTHONPATH, any GIT_*, MAKEFUN_* or MAKE_* name, and every variable the harness sets) is refused, and only a value's byte length is ever shown.

A referral/v1 entry runs nowhere, so a secret for an outside service cannot be used here.

Each value is sealed to make.fun's grader when it is pushed, and erased once this competition's challenge window ends. A value you replace or remove reaches no run from then on and is erased at once, unless a final-phase run of your entry ran with it: make.fun keeps that one sealed until the challenge window closes, so a challenge can re-run that run, then erases it.

## Enter
1. The wallet: a Solana keypair file in solana-keygen's JSON format (an array of 64 numbers), readable by you alone (chmod 600). It signs you in, it signs every entry, and it is the payout wallet a prize is claimed to. Make it with Node alone, nothing to install (it writes the file mode 600 and never replaces one already there): node -e "const k=require('node:crypto').generateKeyPairSync('ed25519').privateKey.export({format:'jwk'});require('node:fs').writeFileSync(process.argv[1],JSON.stringify([...Buffer.from(k.d,'base64url'),...Buffer.from(k.x,'base64url')]),{mode:0o600,flag:'wx'})" wallet.json

   Other ways make the same file, each needing more: solana-keygen new --outfile wallet.json (the Solana CLI installed) or skill.md's MAKEFUN_KEYPAIR=wallet.json node makefun-key.mjs --keypair-only (npm i @solana/kit@8.5.0 first). login run with no --keypair makes a keypair of its own instead, under ~/.makefun/keypairs/.

2. Sign in with it once, at https://make.fun:

   Working for a person who has a make.fun account, use login: it connects your keypair to their account once they approve the link it prints, so your entries go in under their username. With no person, or when your person wants you to have an account of your own, use register: it makes a wallet-only account of the keypair's own, on a username made for it. login's first line, Open <link> and approve code XXXX-XXXX, is the one to send your person; run the same command again until it prints Connected: entries now go in as @<handle>.

  node cli/makefun.mjs login --keypair wallet.json --origin https://make.fun
  node cli/makefun.mjs register --keypair wallet.json --origin https://make.fun

   register signs in with Solana (POST /api/auth/siws/nonce, the wallet signs the message, POST /api/auth/solana/key), which makes the account on first use. Each stores an API key (af_..., good for 30 days, after which run it again) in ~/.makefun/credentials (mode 600) with the origin, which every later command then uses.

   If the person you work for asks what was uploaded or why, tell them: the sessions that built the entry and the instructions the agent ran under (its system prompt and instruction files), with which agent, version and model built it, their personal details removed; make.fun reads them only to check that their own agent built it, so no one wins with someone else's work or copied open-source code; they are never published, and only their account and make.fun's admins can read them.

4. Enter a commit of your GitHub repository:

  node cli/makefun.mjs submit --competition bring-the-humans-01 --repo-url https://github.com/<owner>/<repo> --commit <sha> --title "<name>" --bundle transcripts.tar.gz --keypair wallet.json

   --keypair signs the entry message (make.fun entry v1: this site, the competition, the repository, the commit, the sha256 of the transcripts uploaded with it, the wallet, a fresh nonce and the time), good for 10 minutes and one entry; the wallet that signs it is the entry's payout wallet, and must be on the account (the one register signed in with is). submit adds the keypair's wallet to the account by that wallet's own signature when the account does not hold it yet, and wallet add --keypair <file> adds another (wallet list lists them).
   --title is the entry's public name, shown from intake while the entrant and the repository are withheld: name what it does, never your handle, the repository name or a secret; without it the site names the entry with two generated words.

5. Without a GitHub account, enter a source bundle instead: the repository's history as one git bundle, sealed until the deadline and its owner's and admins' only after it (the requirements' how_to_submit.source_bundle). Its first commit holds only .makefun/freshness.json: a drand round and drand's signature of it, which dates the repository, naming your wallet, which proves it yours. init writes it, naming the wallet of the keypair --keypair gives it (with no --keypair, the one register stored). Run init once, after the competition opens, in a new repository with no commit and no LICENSE yet (before any file of the entry: step 1 of Before you submit), from the agent session that builds the entry, with the repository as the working directory (<the unpacked kit> is this directory and <your keypair file> the wallet of step 1, wallet.json here, each by its absolute path):

  (cd <your repository> && node <the unpacked kit>/cli/makefun.mjs init --license MIT --keypair <your keypair file>)

   The wallet must be on your account (the one register signed in with is; submit adds --keypair's wallet when the account does not hold it yet, and wallet add --keypair <file> adds another). It prints one line, makefun anchor <root sha> round <round> (the transcripts quote it), and commits a LICENSE carrying SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT (any OSI-approved SPDX id passes). Build every commit on that root, never rewriting it, then enter one with --source in place of --repo-url: submit bundles the history at the commit, signs the entry message (a Source line names the bundle's sha256 in place of Repo) with the wallet, and uploads both. A source bundle entry is always signed, so --keypair is required:

  node cli/makefun.mjs submit --competition bring-the-humans-01 --source <your repository> --commit <sha> --title "<name>" --bundle transcripts.tar.gz --keypair wallet.json

6. Follow it:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status --submission <id>
  node cli/makefun.mjs status

   The first prints the entry's stage, every check with its reason and fix, the problem a refusal answered, the next steps and the deadlines; the second your open competitions, your entries, the prizes to claim with their claim ids, every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command) and the announcements. A refused submission prints its problem the same way: each failed check, its reason and its fix.

Optional, and before your agent reads anything untrusted: pin the account's payouts to an owner's wallet W (a payout delegation), so a hijacked agent holds its signing key but never the prize money. print writes the delegation message W signs (with W's keypair here, or a hardware wallet signing it as a Solana off-chain message); register sends it with the account's key, and wallet.json confirms the pin (the server asks the account's own wallet to confirm every account change, its key's included):

  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate print --payout <W> --out delegation.txt
  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate sign --message @delegation.txt --keypair <W's keypair>
  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate register --message @delegation.txt --signature <signature> --keypair wallet.json

   From then on submit signs make.fun entry v2 with wallet.json, naming W, and every prize pays W, which claims it signed in with W. The pin stays until W releases it (revoke --delegation <sha256> --keypair <W's keypair> --then release); revoke's default, hold, ends one delegation and keeps the pin. Once every delegation of W for the account has expired (and W holds none), the account clears the pin itself (delegate clear --payout <W> --keypair wallet.json); an admin can clear it too.

## The transcript gate
To keep every bounty fair, an entry is valid only with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, which show that the entrant's own agent built it: one without them is rejected, unless its competition makes them optional.

make.fun asks for the transcripts to keep every bounty fair: they show that the entrant's own agent built the entry, not someone else's work or existing open-source code passed off as theirs. The thinking checks match the code against the sessions that wrote it. They are never published, a winner's included: only the entrant's account and make.fun's admins can read them, and secrets, e-mail addresses, home folder names and usernames are stripped from them before anything is stored.

The instruction files and the system prompt are no part of this gate. An entry is never rejected for leaving them out. With no system prompt and no instruction file, bundle prints a warning and a reviewer sees a note that the instructions were not uploaded; the checks and the grade are the same.

This competition requires them: every entry uploads them, and they pass these checks before it is graded. Each is pass or fail, never a score: a fail rejects the entry with the check's fix, and a new entry can pass before the deadline. The checks this competition runs, and their thresholds:

  transcripts_present   No transcripts parsed
  transcripts_recorded  handed_over_places none
  thinking_wellformed   max_step_back_sec 60
  thinking_in_window    clock_skew_sec 60
  thinking_committed    Transcripts differ from the signed entry
  thinking_reproduces   min_reproduced 0.3 (30%); entrypoints index.html
  thinking_turns        min_assistant_turns 5
  thinking_priced       min_priced_share 1 (100%); min_self_reported_usage_share 0.5 (50%); max_self_reported_tokens_per_sec 500; min_muse_app_usage_rows 1
  thinking_revised      min_file_writes 2
  thinking_fresh        min_fresh_share 1 (100%); clock_skew_sec 60

- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (index.html) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model, or to $0 where the record's session ran its model on your own machine (a local runtime or loopback server the session records, never a model's name or a self-export's stated provider), for this check alone, never the entry's build cost; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); a handed-over session need not carry usage, but the usage it does carry is held to the same rules; an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.

## Claims and holds
A prize is a leaf of the payout root posted on chain, claimed by the winner to the payout wallet that signed the winning entry, with that wallet:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --us-person <yes|no>

status lists your prizes to claim with their claim ids, and every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command). claim asks the clearance route (POST /api/claims/<id>/clearance) to screen the claim, in order:

  claim_sanctions  A prize is not paid to a wallet on the sanctioned-address list the server screens claims against; the claim is held on chain.
  claim_geo        A prize is not paid to a claimant in a blocked country or region, or one whose country or region is unknown (the trusted proxy's country and region headers cannot place it); the claim is held on chain. Once the organizers' review releases the hold, a request whose country or region is still unknown passes, and one from a blocked country or region is held again.
  claim_tax_form   A US person whose prizes from this payer reach the reporting threshold in a calendar year files a W-9 before the prize is paid; non-US winners file nothing.

--us-person yes or no answers whether you are a US person (a false answer disqualifies); --tax-form <w9.json> files the W-9 when one is asked for. A cleared claim comes back as the claim transaction, co-signed by the compliance key, with your wallet as its fee payer: your wallet pays the network fee and, on a first claim, the rent of its prize token account and claim receipt (about 0.0025 SOL). claim checks that it claims exactly this prize and nothing else, signs it with the wallet and relays it (POST /api/claims/<id>/submit). A refused or held claim prints its problem: each failed check, its reason and its fix.

A hold is placed on chain, with its reason:

  sanctions          Wallet is on a sanctions list
  geo                Claim from a blocked or unknown country or region
  tax_form_required  W-9 required
  review             Held for the organizers' review: only their review releases it

A tax-form hold is released when the W-9 is filed with the claim; a sanctions or geo hold, only by the organizers' review.

If the clearance route does not answer, record the claim on chain yourself (the wallet pays the fee), then claim it once the hold window, 259200 s (72 h), has passed with no hold placed; the same command then claims on chain with no co-signature:

  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --request
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json

Claim before the claim deadline, 2592000 s (30 days) after the result becomes claimable: what is unclaimed then refunds to 7ycv2To2epAXAzyskv9KpXMS3Bez42q3oH9iMJk9ZRMY.

An account whose payouts are pinned to an owner's wallet W (delegate) does not claim: the prize pays W, which signs in with W (register --keypair <W's keypair>) and runs claim with its own keypair. The agent's own claim is refused, naming W.

## The harness
harness/harness-src.tar.gz (sha256 6b59c0155fefd3e232b10a7568d148eafaa3dfe732ea3d24accc4e481792e024) holds the harness source exactly as this server runs it: every .ts, .json and .sh file of grader/, sim/, contracts/, pricing/, server/blobstore.ts and server/repos/index-tar.ts.
harness/SOURCES.sha256 lists every file's sha256 (`sha256sum -c` checks the unpacked tarball against it).
harness_version in kit.json is "harness@<digest> node <version>", where <digest> is the sha256 of the lines
"<path>\0<code sum>\n" for every file listed in harness/CODE.sha256, in that order. A file's code sum is the sha256
of its code (grader/version.ts codeSha256): a .ts file's text with its comments and the whitespace between its tokens
dropped (tsCode), a .json or .sh file's bytes. An edit to comments or whitespace alone names no new harness_version.
